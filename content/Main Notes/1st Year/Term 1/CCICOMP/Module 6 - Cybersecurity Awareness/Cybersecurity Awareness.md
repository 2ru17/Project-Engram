## Overview

This document provides an introduction to cybersecurity awareness, focusing on understanding common cyber threats, reviewing major real-life cyber incidents, and establishing best practices for digital hygiene. It also highlights the critical importance of ethical behavior in computing and the role of ethical hacking in identifying vulnerabilities.

## Key Definitions
 **Cybersecurity:** The practice of defending computers, servers, mobile devices, electronic systems, networks, and data from malicious digital attacks. **Malware (e.g., Ransomware):** Malicious software designed to disrupt, damage, or gain unauthorized access to a computer system. *Ransomware* encrypts user data and demands payment for the decryption key. **Botnet:** A network of private computers infected with malicious software and controlled as a group without the owners' knowledge (e.g., used for DDoS attacks). **Phishing:** A cybercrime where targets are contacted by email, telephone, or text message by someone posing as a legitimate institution to lure individuals into providing sensitive data. **Ethical Hacking:** The practice of intentionally probing computer systems to find security vulnerabilities that a malicious hacker could potentially exploit, generally done with permission (e.g., Bug Bounty Programs).

## Theoretical Framework

### Notable Real-Life Cyber Attacks
Understanding historical attacks helps in recognizing threat vectors: **WannaCry (2017):** A massive global ransomware attack that targeted computers running the Microsoft Windows operating system by encrypting data and demanding ransom payments in the Bitcoin cryptocurrency. **Mirai Botnet (2016):** A malware that turns networked devices running Linux into remotely controlled bots that can be used as part of a botnet in large-scale network attacks. **Yahoo Data Breach (2013-2014):** One of the largest data breaches in history, compromising billions of user accounts. **SolarWinds Supply Chain Attack (2020):** Hackers inserted malicious code into the SolarWinds Orion IT monitoring system, compromising thousands of organizations, including US government agencies. **GCash Phishing Scam (2024):** Targeted social engineering attacks aiming to steal user credentials and funds from the popular mobile wallet.

### Best Practices and Cyber Hygiene
To mitigate the risk of cyber threats, users and organizations should adopt the following hygiene tips: Regularly update operating systems and software to patch vulnerabilities. Utilize strong, unique passwords and enable Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA). Be cautious of unsolicited communications containing links or attachments (anti-phishing). Implement regular, secure data backups.

### Ethical Responsibilities in Computing
Professionals in computing hold ethical responsibilities to safeguard user data and maintain system integrity. **Case Study:** Ethical hackers participating in Bug Bounty Programs. By responsibly disclosing vulnerabilities to organizations rather than exploiting them or selling them on the black market, ethical hackers play a vital role in strengthening overall cybersecurity posture.

    - **A)** Disabling all firewalls to improve internet speed.
